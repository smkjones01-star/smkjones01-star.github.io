The Case for a Slower Life

On productivity, perfectionism, and why doing less might actually be the most radical thing we can do right now.

By Sydney Jones

There is a particular kind of pressure that arrives disguised as possibility. There are always more things to read, more places to go, more work to do, more ways to become a slightly improved version of yourself.

For a while, this can feel energizing. Eventually, though, the horizon stops looking like freedom and starts looking like a list.

I have been thinking about what it would mean to resist that instinct—not by abandoning ambition, but by refusing to let every hour become evidence of whether I used my life correctly.
